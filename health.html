02 / Why it matters

The risk can be quiet and cumulative.

A person may feel well while consuming chemically contaminated water. Microbial illness can appear quickly; some chemical effects develop after years of exposure.

Safe water is essential to health · Punjab, India

Exposure & wellbeing

Different hazards.
Different timelines.

The possible effect depends on the contaminant, its concentration, how long a person is exposed, and individual vulnerability. A water test—not symptoms—can help identify a chemical risk.

DAYS

Immediate illness

Waterborne microbes may trigger diarrhoea, vomiting or fever. Infants and older adults can be especially vulnerable to dehydration.

YEARS

Effects that accumulate

Repeated exposure to certain chemicals can harm organs or affect teeth, bones and other systems over time.

NOW

Prevention is possible

Test the source, choose a treatment matched to results, and verify that it keeps working.

Protecting people

Prevention starts
before symptoms.

Do not rely on taste, smell, clarity or the absence of illness to judge chemical safety. If a test identifies a concern, follow local public-health guidance and provide a verified safe source for drinking and cooking while next steps are arranged.

How exposure affects the body

Risk depends on the
dose and the duration.

These are potential health effects described in public-health guidance, not predictions about any one person's health.

MICROBIAL · SHORT TERM

Infection & dehydration

Pathogenic bacteria, viruses and parasites can cause diarrhoea and other gastrointestinal illness. Young children, older adults and people with weakened immunity can become seriously dehydrated more quickly.

Disinfection can reduce many microbial risks, but a contaminated source, dirty storage vessel or unsafe handling can re-contaminate water after treatment.

NITRATE · INFANT VULNERABILITY

Reduced oxygen carrying

Very high nitrate exposure can cause methemoglobinemia (“blue baby syndrome”) in infants, where haemoglobin carries oxygen less effectively. Formula preparation water is an important possible exposure route.

Boiling does not remove nitrate and can raise its concentration as water evaporates. Use a verified safe source and seek urgent medical care if an infant is unwell.

ARSENIC · LONG-TERM

Skin, cardiovascular & cancer risks

Years of drinking water with elevated arsenic can cause skin pigmentation changes and thickening, and is associated with cancers including skin, bladder and lung cancer. Other non-cancer effects have also been reported.

Effects may take years to appear; no symptoms does not establish that water is safe. Confirm arsenic using an appropriate laboratory test.

FLUORIDE · LONG-TERM

Dental & skeletal fluorosis

Excess fluoride intake while teeth are developing can cause dental fluorosis. Higher exposure sustained over many years can damage bones and joints (skeletal fluorosis).

Fluoride is also beneficial at appropriate levels for dental health. The concern is excessive total intake, so assess water results and local health advice rather than assuming “zero is best.”

URANIUM · CHEMICAL TOXICITY

Kidney effects

For uranium in drinking water, chemical toxicity—particularly effects on the kidneys—is the main public-health concern at relevant exposure levels. Its presence cannot be inferred from water taste or a household TDS meter.

Request uranium analysis if advised by the local authority or risk assessment, and interpret results against the applicable drinking-water guidance.

MORE THAN ONE EXPOSURE

Shared water, shared protection

People may drink from a private well, a public supply and bottled water at different times. Cooking and infant formula can also contribute to exposure.

Keep a record of the source used, test date, treatment and maintenance. Share verified results with the local water authority so nearby households can receive appropriate follow-up.
